A Trellis for Your Soul

Just as a trellis provides structure for a plant to grow, Seed offers a framework for spiritual formation. This is a disciple's way—practicing the ways of Jesus so we can experience the life of Jesus.

Growth doesn't happen overnight. It requires patience, intentionality, and consistent tending. Whether you're deepening your prayer life, meditating on scripture, or practicing silence, Seed helps you build sustainable rhythms as you partner with the Holy Spirit in the work of forming you into the image of Jesus.

Seed is free for everyone—a gift to support your journey of spiritual formation.

Start small. Tend faithfully. Watch what God grows.

About My Heart Soil

I created My Heart Soil as a pastor who is learning, alongside others, what it means to faithfully follow Jesus. With their help, we made this to help you follow Him too.

Over the years, I've walked with people who sincerely want to grow as disciples but often feel stretched, distracted, or unsure how real transformation happens. I've experienced that same tension in my own life. It is easy to focus on doing the right things while overlooking the condition of our hearts.

Jesus often described growth in quiet, ordinary ways. In Mark 4, He speaks of the heart as soil where the Word is received and cultivated. In John 15, He invites His disciples to abide in Him so that fruit can grow naturally over time.

To abide means to remain with Jesus. Like a branch connected to the vine, we do not strain to produce fruit. We stay connected. Life flows from Jesus into us through prayer, Scripture, trust, and obedience. Over time, His life produces fruit that we could never manufacture on our own.

My Heart Soil exists to help followers of Jesus slow down, stay present with Him, and pay attention to what is happening beneath the surface. The practices here are simple and rooted in Scripture, creating space to listen, reflect, and respond to Jesus.

This space is built on trust. Trusting Jesus to do His work in us. We are not after more information, but transformation. Not more seed, but better soil.
